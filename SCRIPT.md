HOOSHANG  
a play in two acts

Adapted from the video game Hooshang  
(dialogue and story only — the platforming and puzzles that carry them  
in the original are not part of this text)

CHARACTERS

HOOSHANG — An elderly office worker; later, in memory, a boy of about ten.  
RUMI — A golden, glowing guide who appears to Hooshang inside his own mind.  
DARKSHANG — A living shadow: Hooshang's own long-fed fear, given shape and set loose. Never speaks.  
JAMSHID — Hooshang's cousin, a boy, met again in the memory of a summer in Iran.

SETTING

Act One takes place entirely inside a dim, endless corporate office — which is revealed, room by room, to be a landscape built out of Hooshang's own mind. Act Two is a warm, sun-drenched memory of a childhood pond in Iran.

\==========================================================  
ACT ONE — THE OFFICE  
\==========================================================

ONE — WAKING

(Black. Then, slowly, light. A dim, endless landscape of office cubicles under flickering fluorescent tubes. HOOSHANG — an old man in rumpled office clothes — stirs on the floor, coming round.)

HOOSHANG  
Where am I?

(He looks one way down the row of cubicles, then the other, taking it in.)

HOOSHANG  
This doesn't feel like my cubicle...

TWO — THE MEETING

(RUMI, a figure made of soft gold light, appears ahead of him.)

(Hooshang starts.)

HOOSHANG  
Hello??

HOOSHANG  
Who are you? I've worked in this office fifteen years. I've never seen you once.

(Rumi says nothing. He only stands there, glowing gently. A long, unhurried silence.)

HOOSHANG  
Are you going to say something, or just stand there glowing at me...

HOOSHANG  
...I think I hit my head harder than I thought.

(A pause, before Rumi finally answers.)

RUMI  
You stand at the beginning of your most important journey, Hooshang jaan.

HOOSHANG  
A journey? I just wanted to make it to my car.

RUMI  
You will not reach the parking lot from here. This is not that building. It is the one you carry inside you.

HOOSHANG  
...Inside me?

RUMI  
Your mind has built its own rooms, and you must go down through every one.

RUMI  
The office that swallowed your years. Your childhood. And beneath them, the places and things you have never let go of.

HOOSHANG  
And I'm supposed to just walk through my own head.

RUMI  
Not walk through. You have done that your whole life.

RUMI  
To pass, you must meet what waits in each room.

RUMI  
They are not memories, jaan. They are still alive. And they will not let you by until you face them.

HOOSHANG  
Look — this really isn't a good time. I just lost my job. I'll get to all that... later.

RUMI  
...Later. Yes. It's always later, isn't it.

RUMI  
You have knocked on this door your whole life, from the inside. Now it opens.

(Rumi fades from sight. Hooshang is alone again — but the way ahead, for the first time, is open.)

THREE — THE GIFT

(He walks on, deeper into the office, until the floor gives way beneath him and he is left hanging over a drop with no way up and no way across. RUMI appears again, beside him in the air. Light passes between them.)

RUMI  
Hold the way you want to go — up and onward — and dash\!

(Hooshang tries it, and for the first time in his life, does.)

FOUR — THOUGHTS YOU'D RATHER NOT HAVE

(Further in, a small black cloud drifts through the dark, a hot red rim around its edge. RUMI is suddenly beside him again.)

RUMI  
These are the thoughts you would rather not have, Hooshang jaan.

RUMI  
You cannot strike them down. There is nothing here to strike.

RUMI  
Let it come, watch it travel, and step where it is not.

FIVE — THOUGHTS YOU LIKE

(A second cloud drifts by, this one pale and warm.)

RUMI  
And these, jaan, are the thoughts you like.

HOOSHANG  
...These ones are going to kill me too, aren't they?

RUMI  
Examine them and you will find they are made of the same thing.

RUMI  
Craving is the heavier of the two. Fear, at least, you already know you shouldn't chase.

SIX — BOTH, AND NEITHER

(Both kinds of cloud now, moving together.)

RUMI  
Now both. Watch how they travel. One rises and falls. One crosses. One circles you in the dark.

RUMI  
Do not sort them, jaan. Pleasant, unpleasant — both are only weather.

RUMI  
Meet them the same way, and the way through opens on its own.

SEVEN — THE TILES

(A stretch of the office he cannot see across — dark, silent, without a single light.)

HOOSHANG  
I can't see a thing.

(Rumi appears, faint, glowing dimly at the far edge of the dark.)

HOOSHANG  
You show up right before the parts I'm going to hate, don't you?

RUMI  
The dark awaits you; you think you've lost your rhythm. You haven't. It's only sleeping — here, in these tiles.

RUMI  
Wake the notes in their order, one after the next, until they join into a melody. Complete it, and its light will gather on you — enough to carry you through the dark ahead.

HOOSHANG  
So I play the tune, I get a light... Fine.

EIGHT — THE SHADOW

(A cold blue moon hangs in a window behind them. Something is standing in the dark ahead — a shape cut entirely out of shadow.)

HOOSHANG  
What is that??

RUMI  
That is the seed you have sown and watered, jaan. Water a bad thought faithfully enough, and it grows tall enough to shut out the light.

HOOSHANG  
Meaning... every time the thought came, I fed it. And it grew.

RUMI  
Yes. It wears the face of total darkness.

RUMI  
No one can fight this for you. Not even me.

RUMI  
Don't let it absorb you.

(Behind them, the moon in the window goes dark — and comes back blood red. The shadow, DARKSHANG, begins to move.)

NINE — THE LONG WAY HOME

(Wordless. HOOSHANG runs. Behind him, the office is coming apart, room by room — ceiling panels drop away, brick shakes loose from the walls, the whole building groaning as if it, too, is afraid. DARKSHANG is always behind him: if hooshang moves too slow, hesitates or backtracks he is absobped. 

TEN — THE END OF THE CHASE

(Hooshang makes it to his cubicle (where the game first started) Sunrise light is coming through a window nearby. Darkshang slowly vanishes.)

HOOSHANG  
(a small, startled breath)  
It's... gone?

(RUMI returns, standing between Hooshang and the window — already in the light.)

RUMI  
No, jaan. A darkness grown this strong cannot be defeated overnight. But stop watering it, and it stops growing.

HOOSHANG  
So I never had to defeat it. I just had to stop feeding it.

RUMI  
Yes. The roots remain. It may green again someday, if ever you water it. But you know the way of it now — you need only stop. Let the thought come, let it stand, let it go. Do not water it with your fear, and it withers on its own.

RUMI  
Come. Let us see where this seed came from -- its origin story.

(Rumi's own glow rises and swells until it swallows the whole stage in gold.)

                    ACT II · CHILDHOOD, IRAN

==========================================================
ACT TWO — THE POND
==========================================================

ONE — THE POND

(Bright sun. A pond, and on its far bank, a boy sits fishing — JAMSHID. HOOSHANG, himself a boy now in this memory, swims across toward him.)

JAMSHID
Cousin joon!

HOOSHANG
Jamshid! It's so good to see you!

JAMSHID
You saw me yesterday.

HOOSHANG
It feels like so much longer.

JAMSHID
Well, get out of the water. You're frightening dinner.

(Hooshang climbs up onto the bank beside him.)

HOOSHANG
Are the fish biting today?

JAMSHID
They would if little kids stopped swimming through their house.

(A splash — something takes the line.)

JAMSHID
Finally!!

(Jamshid reels in a fish, triumphant.)

TWO — THE CAMPFIRE

(Sunset. The fish cooks over a small fire the boys have built.)

HOOSHANG
Does my mother know we're here?

JAMSHID
Yes. She sent snacks.

HOOSHANG
Did she send that pomegranate fruit roll up? Where is it!? 

JAMSHID
You were late.

HOOSHANG (frustrated)
Jamshid

JAMSHID
Fishing is hungry work. She also said to be home before dark...

(The sky deepens toward night. Stars come out overhead.)

JAMSHID
You're quiet today.

HOOSHANG
I had a strange dream.

JAMSHID
Tell me.

HOOSHANG
I was old.

JAMSHID
Did you have a moustache?

HOOSHANG
Yes.

JAMSHID
Good. Go on.

HOOSHANG
I worked in an office. I didn't like it, but I stayed for years. Then I lost the job... and I felt lost, too.

JAMSHID
What happened?

HOOSHANG
Rumi appeared.

JAMSHID
Rumi, the poet, from school?

HOOSHANG
Yes. He taught me how to stay equanimous with my thoughts.

JAMSHID
You'd better start from the beginning.

(And Hooshang tells him everything — the office, the gift of the dash, the thoughts that came and went like weather, the shadow he had grown himself, and how it finally stopped. The night turns overhead as he talks, the fire burning low, until —)

THREE — DAWN

(The sky has gone pale with morning.)

HOOSHANG
...and then I woke up here.

JAMSHID
What a dream.

HOOSHANG
Do you remember yours?

JAMSHID
Almost never. By the time I wake up, they're gone.

HOOSHANG
This one felt like I'd lived it.

JAMSHID
No wonder you looked so tired.

(A pause.)

HOOSHANG
I'm glad you're here.

JAMSHID
I'm glad you came down.

(Another pause.)

JAMSHID
Next time you're old, come looking for me.

(A school bell rings, far off.)

JAMSHID
That can't be the school bell.

HOOSHANG
We talked all night.

(The bell rings again.)

JAMSHID
Come over after school. We can finish talking.

(Jamshid runs off down the bank, jumps — and lands square on a magic carpet passing low over the water, which sails on without so much as slowing.)

HOOSHANG
That's how you get to school?

JAMSHID
How do you think I keep arriving before you?

(The carpet rises and carries him off over the trees, out of sight.)

FOUR — THE WALL

(The carpet disappears over the trees. The SCHOOL BELL keeps ringing.)

(Once. Twice. Again.)

(With each ring, the pond changes. One summer passes, then another. The boys grow taller in flashes. Fishing poles become bicycles. School clothes become teenage clothes. The warm little pond recedes behind apartment blocks, traffic, painted signs, and the restless streets of Tehran.)

(By the final bell, HOOSHANG and JAMSHID are seventeen.)

(Tehran, late 1970s — though not quite Tehran as it was. This is Tehran remembered by someone who has spent a lifetime trying not to remember it. The colors are too warm. The sky is too blue. Every shop sign seems hand-painted. Every passing car gives a cheerful little HONK. Somewhere, music plays from a radio that cannot be found.)

(HOOSHANG stands before a long white wall with a spray can in his hand, staring at himself as though surprised to find it there.)

JAMSHID
Cousin joon!

(HOOSHANG turns. JAMSHID is balanced on the wall above him, grinning.)

JAMSHID
You coming, or are you planning to stare at that wall until we're thirty?

HOOSHANG
I'm coming.

JAMSHID
Good. We haven't committed the crime yet.

(He hops down. At their feet: three spray cans, a paper bag, bread half eaten, and evidence of an evening spent being seventeen. On the wall are their earlier masterpieces — a football score, a terrible moustache on a painted advertisement, a girl's name crossed out and rewritten twice.)

HOOSHANG
Your moustache is crooked.

JAMSHID
It's political commentary.

HOOSHANG
On what?

JAMSHID
Moustaches.

(Hooshang laughs. For a moment there is nothing in the world except the two of them.)

(Jamshid shakes another can.)

JAMSHID
Come on. One good one before we go.

HOOSHANG
My mother said home before dark.

JAMSHID
It is dark somewhere.

HOOSHANG
That is not how mothers work.

JAMSHID
Fine. Something quick.

(He considers the wall, suddenly serious.)

JAMSHID
Write this.

HOOSHANG
What?

JAMSHID
Rumi.

HOOSHANG
We get one last wall and you want homework?

JAMSHID
Just write.

(Hooshang begins painting in Persian. Jamshid speaks the words as they appear.)

JAMSHID
“This world is a prison, and we are prisoners...”

HOOSHANG
Cheerful.

JAMSHID
Keep going.

(Hooshang moves to begin the second line.)

JAMSHID
“Dig through the prison and—”

(A SHRILL WHISTLE.)

(The whole world stops.)

(A uniformed OFFICER stands at the mouth of the alley. In Hooshang's memory he is almost comically severe: enormous moustache, polished boots, whistle much too large for his face.)

(Hooshang's hand opens.)

(The spray can drops.)

(CLACK.)

(For the first time, the sound does not belong to the warm cartoon world.)

(The officer looks at the can. Then at the unfinished line on the wall. Then at Hooshang.)

(Hooshang freezes.)

(Jamshid looks at him.)

(A beat.)

(Jamshid quietly gathers the other spray cans with his foot and pulls them toward himself.)

JAMSHID
Mine.

HOOSHANG
Jamshid—

JAMSHID
All of it.

(The officer takes Jamshid by the arm.)

(Hooshang finally moves.)

HOOSHANG
Wait—

(Jamshid looks back at him.)

JAMSHID
Go home, cousin joon.

(A bright, cheerful CHIME cuts him off.)

(A giant red stamp drops out of the sky and SLAMS across the whole world.)

                    JAMSHID GOT DETENTION!

(Hooshang stares.)

HOOSHANG
Detention?

(The officer walks Jamshid away.)

(With every step, the officer becomes less real: boots too shiny, hat too tall, movements too stiff — until he is almost a wind-up toy soldier.)

(Jamshid looks back once.)

(The scene wipes itself away before Hooshang can hold onto the image.)

FIVE — THE PALACE OF CORRECTIONS

(A ridiculous fortress rises where the neighborhood had been: half schoolhouse, half castle, half government office — confidently containing three halves.)

(A banner drops over the gate.)

                    PALACE OF CORRECTIONS
                    NO RUNNING
                    NO SHOUTING
                    NO POETRY

(A little brass bell rings.)

(A sign falls from the sky.)

                    MAIN QUEST UPDATED:
                    BREAK JAMSHID OUT!

(Beneath it, four empty keyholes appear.)

                    KEYS: 0 / 4

HOOSHANG
Four keys.

(A cheerful CHIME.)

HOOSHANG
Of course there are four keys.

(The gate opens.)

SIX — THE FOUR KEYS

(Ari will design these later)

                    KEY ACQUIRED!
                    KEYS: 4 / 4

                    ALL KEYS FOUND!
                    RESCUE JAMSHID!

HOOSHANG
I'm coming, cousin.

ELEVEN — THE CELL



(A corridor lined with doors.)

(Hooshang uses the first key.)

CLICK.

(The second.)

CLICK.

(The third.)

CLICK.

(The fourth.)

CLICK.

(The final door swings open.)

(JAMSHID is sitting inside.)

(He looks exactly as he did at the wall. Not a day older. Not hurt. Not frightened.)

HOOSHANG
Jamshid.

JAMSHID
Took you long enough.

(Hooshang laughs — once — and then crosses the room and throws his arms around him.)

(Jamshid is surprised by the force of it.)

JAMSHID
Hooshang?

HOOSHANG
Come on.

JAMSHID
Where?

HOOSHANG
Home.

JAMSHID
Oh.

HOOSHANG
Your mother is going to kill both of us.

JAMSHID
Probably just you.

HOOSHANG
Fine. She can kill me after dinner.

(Jamshid smiles.)

HOOSHANG
Come on.

(They go.)

TWELVE — THE PARADE

(The gates burst open.)

(The city erupts in celebration.)

(Confetti. Trumpets. Children waving little flags. Shopkeepers cheering. Even the toy guards applaud. A banner unfurls across the sky.)

                    JAMSHID RESCUED!

(Hooshang looks up at it.)

(He begins to cry.)

JAMSHID
Why are you crying?

HOOSHANG
I'm not.

JAMSHID
You are.

HOOSHANG
I'm tired.

JAMSHID
You always cry when you're tired?

HOOSHANG
Apparently.

(Jamshid puts an arm around him.)

JAMSHID
You got me out, cousin joon.

HOOSHANG
Yeah.

JAMSHID
You found all four keys.

HOOSHANG
Yeah.

(They walk on through the celebration.)

(The painted wall from the beginning appears ahead of them.)

(The first line is still there.)

                    THIS WORLD IS A PRISON,
                    AND WE ARE PRISONERS—

(The second line remains unfinished.)

(Hooshang slows.)

HOOSHANG
We never finished it.

JAMSHID
No.

HOOSHANG
We can finish it now.

(Jamshid looks at the wall.)

JAMSHID
No.

HOOSHANG
Why not?

(A school bell rings somewhere far away.)

(Jamshid looks toward the sound.)

JAMSHID
That's mine.

THIRTEEN — THE CARPET

(A magic carpet glides around the corner and settles beside Jamshid as naturally as a bus pulling to a curb.)

(Hooshang laughs with relief.)

HOOSHANG
There it is.

JAMSHID
There it is.

HOOSHANG
Come on. We'll take it back.

(Jamshid steps onto the carpet.)

HOOSHANG
Move over.

(Jamshid doesn't.)

HOOSHANG
Jamshid.

JAMSHID
You go home.

HOOSHANG
What?

JAMSHID
Your mother said before dark.

HOOSHANG
It's morning.

JAMSHID
Then you're very late.

HOOSHANG
Stop.

(Jamshid's smile softens.)

JAMSHID
Cousin joon—

HOOSHANG
We're going home.

JAMSHID
You are.

HOOSHANG
We just got you out.

JAMSHID
I know.

HOOSHANG
I found all four keys.

JAMSHID
I know.

HOOSHANG
So get off the carpet.

(A pause.)

JAMSHID
I can't.

(Hooshang laughs once because the alternative is understanding him.)

HOOSHANG
What do you mean you can't?

(The carpet begins to rise.)

HOOSHANG
No. No, no — Jamshid, get off.

(Hooshang grabs his hand.)

(The celebration continues around them, but the sound has become distant and warped.)

HOOSHANG
Get off!

JAMSHID
Hooshang.

HOOSHANG
I got you out!

JAMSHID
Yes.

HOOSHANG
Then stay!

(The carpet rises higher. Hooshang hangs from Jamshid's hand.)

(Somewhere in the air, bright letters appear.)

                    HOLD ON

(Hooshang holds tighter.)

HOOSHANG
I'm not letting go.

(The city flickers.)

(For one instant, Jamshid is not on a carpet.)

(He is seventeen in the alley, an officer's hand around his arm.)

(Then the carpet.)

HOOSHANG
I can fix it.

JAMSHID
You tried.

HOOSHANG
I can fix it!

(The city flickers again.)

(A government counter.)

MAN'S VOICE
There is no one here by that name.

(The carpet.)

                    HOLD ON

HOOSHANG
Tell me what I missed.

JAMSHID
Hooshang—

HOOSHANG
There has to be another key.

(Jamshid looks at him.)

JAMSHID
There weren't any keys.

(The music stops.)

FOURTEEN — WHAT HAPPENED

(The colors drain out of the world.)

(The magic carpet hangs in darkness.)

(Hooshang still has Jamshid's hand.)

HOOSHANG
Don't.

(A flash.)

(The real alley.)

(The spray can on the ground.)

OFFICER
Who painted it?

(Hooshang, seventeen, silent.)

JAMSHID
Mine.

(A flash.)

(Jamshid being led away.)

JAMSHID
Go home, cousin joon.

(A flash.)

(Jamshid's mother in her doorway.)

JAMSHID'S MOTHER
Where is my son?

(A flash.)

(A counter. A clerk who will not look up.)

CLERK
No record.

(A flash.)

(A prison wall at night.)

(Hooshang outside it.)

(A flash.)

(The wall from the alley. Rain has begun to wash the paint down.)

                    THIS WORLD IS A PRISON,
                    AND WE ARE PRISONERS—

(The second line still unfinished.)

(Back to the carpet.)

HOOSHANG
I should have said they were mine.

JAMSHID
We both painted it.

HOOSHANG
I should have said something.

JAMSHID
You were seventeen.

HOOSHANG
So were you.

(A long silence.)

HOOSHANG
You were supposed to come home.

(Jamshid looks at him.)

JAMSHID
I know.

HOOSHANG
I waited.

JAMSHID
I know.

HOOSHANG
I left.

JAMSHID
I know.

HOOSHANG
I got old.

(Jamshid smiles, almost amused.)

JAMSHID
I noticed.

(Hooshang laughs through tears.)

HOOSHANG
I wasted it.

(Jamshid says nothing.)

HOOSHANG
All that time.

JAMSHID
Is that why you came back?

(Hooshang cannot answer.)

(The carpet begins, very gently, to pull away.)

                    HOLD ON

(Hooshang tightens his grip.)

JAMSHID
My hand hurts.

(Hooshang looks at their hands.)

(The bright letters change.)

                    LET GO

HOOSHANG
No.

JAMSHID
Hooshang.

HOOSHANG
If I let go, you leave.

JAMSHID
I already left.

(Hooshang breaks.)

(He presses Jamshid's hand to his forehead.)

HOOSHANG
I'm sorry.

JAMSHID
I know.

(Hooshang slowly opens his hand.)

FIFTEEN — THE SECOND LINE

(The carpet rises.)

(Jamshid grows smaller against the pale morning sky.)

(Hooshang stands alone beside the wall.)

(The spray can is still there.)

(He picks it up.)

(The unfinished Rumi verse waits.)

(Hooshang looks at the blank space beneath the first line.)

(He paints.)

                    DIG THROUGH THE PRISON
                    AND SET YOURSELF FREE.

(He steps back.)

(The first sunlight reaches the wall.)

(For just a moment, very high above him, the shape of a magic carpet crosses the sun.)

(Hooshang looks up.)

(He does not chase it.)

END OF ACT TWO

A NOTE ON WHAT COMES NEXT

This is where the game's story stands today. A third act is sketched in the project files — a luminous, sunlit ocean that brightens the deeper Hooshang goes — but it has not yet been written. Where it takes him is not yet known, even to the people making it.
